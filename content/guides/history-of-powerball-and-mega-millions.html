Lotto*America, then Powerball

MUSL dates itself to September 16, 1987. Lotto*America’s first drawing was February 13, 1988, seven from 40 at $1, then six from 54 on February 8, 1989, two plays for $1. It ended April 18, 1992. Powerball went on sale April 19. The April 22 drawing was 5/45 + 1/45, with a $2 million start and the red ball drawn apart from the white balls. The media center names 15 launch lotteries and counts 48 U.S. jurisdictions now.

The Big Game becomes Mega Millions

The Big Game went on sale August 31, 1996. The first drawing was September 6, in Georgia, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, and Virginia. The Washington Post that June reported Friday drawings and a $4 million start. On February 6, 1998, Penelope Kyle of the Virginia Lottery set the first Tuesday drawing for February 10. Michigan described five numbers from 50, nine prizes, a 1 in 22 chance of any prize, and an 11 p.m. draw.

On January 15, 1999 the matrix became 5/50 + 1/36. Michigan first said about 1 in 76 million, then printed 1 in 76,275,360. The start was $5 million, the price stayed $1, and cash joined a 26-payment annuity. New Jersey joined in May 1999. In May 2002 New York and Ohio joined and the name became Mega Millions: sales May 15 at $1, last Big Game drawing May 14, first new drawing May 17. The minimum was $10 million, with overall odds of 1 in 43. The matrix changed too. Texas, on December 5, 2003, sold five from 52 and a Mega Ball from 52.

Cross-selling in 2010

On January 31, 2010 the games went on sale together. Mega Millions says 23 lotteries joined it that day. Tennessee counted 33 jurisdictions at the start. In Michigan, Powerball was $1, drawn Wednesday and Saturday at 10:59 p.m., with a $20 million guarantee. MUSL offered Powerball in Georgia, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, New Jersey, New York, Texas, Virginia, and Washington, 43 lotteries in all, and offered Mega Millions in 23 of its members. Those Mega Millions drawings began February 2. The new Powerball sellers drew on February 3.

On October 22, 2013 the field became 75 and 15, the start rose from $12 million to $15 million, and the jackpot odds were given as 1 in 259 million. The price stayed $1. Five white balls rose from $250,000 to $1 million. Sales at $2 began October 28, 2017, with a $40 million start and any prize at 1 in 24. Idaho’s first drawing was October 31. The March 25, 2025 release priced the April 8 game at $5, after April 4 on the old chart. One fewer Mega Ball moved the jackpot from 1 in 302,575,350 to 1 in 290,472,336, and any prize from 1 in 24 to 1 in 23. A new jackpot starts at $50 million.

Power Play, the Megaplier, and Double Play

Power Play, added March 7, 2001, multiplied every prize except the jackpot by up to five. Florida dates set amounts, up to $2 million, to January 15, 2012, and a 2, 3, 4, or 5 multiplier again to January 19, 2014, with five white balls plus Power Play fixed at $2 million. In 2015, 10× was added under Match 5 when the advertised jackpot is $150 million or less. The jackpot is not multiplied.

Texas added the Megaplier on December 5, 2003: $1 extra, multiplying non-jackpot prizes by 2, 3, or 4. Its file keeps that column through April 4, 2025. In 2013 the consortium priced it at $1 and added a 5, so five white balls could pay $5 million. The March 25, 2025 release removed it, put 2× through 10× in the $5 price, and ended Just the Jackpot. The Mega Millions guide has the rows. Double Play, from August 23, 2021, redraws the same numbers for a $10 million prize, at $1 in the jurisdictions named in that release.

Which nights they are drawn

The Big Game was Fridays only until February 10, 1998, then Tuesday and Friday at 11 p.m. Eastern in Atlanta. Powerball in 2010 was Wednesday and Saturday at 10:59 p.m. August 23, 2021 added Monday at that hour, from Tallahassee. Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday remain the nights.

Jackpots that reset the record

Annuities are before taxes. See the jackpot guide and the largest jackpots page.

Mega Millions reached $656 million on March 30, 2012, split by Illinois, Kansas, and Maryland. Three Powerball tickets shared $1.586 billion in January 2016, the first multi-jurisdictional prize past $1 billion. One South Carolina ticket won $1.537 billion on October 23, 2018, and a Florida ticket won $1.602 billion on August 8, 2023. The media center still calls November 7, 2022 the record: $2.04 billion, one California ticket.
